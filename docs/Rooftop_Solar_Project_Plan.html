Rooftop Solar Potential

A city-scale pipeline that scores every rooftop for solar suitability — the engineering evolution of a Grasshopper + Ladybug prototype into a reproducible data pipeline over an entire city.

Project plan  ·  v1 Washington, DC → v2 India

Where this comes from

This is not a from-scratch portfolio piece. In 2024 I built a working Grasshopper / Rhino + Ladybug prototype (Smart India Hackathon, PS SIH1739) that pulled OpenStreetMap footprints, extruded them into a LOD-1 city model of central New Delhi, ran full-year solar-radiation and inter-building shadow simulation, and computed BIPV potential on both rooftops and facades.

This project scales that instinct — from one neighbourhood in a design tool to an entire city in a reproducible pipeline, trading hand-built LOD-1 blocks for LiDAR-derived per-roof geometry and deep-learning roof segmentation. The prototype already proved the hard part (mutual shading between buildings, §7); this version earns the resolution and the scale.

01

The one-line pitch

Score the rooftop-solar suitability of every building in a city — per-roof geometry, orientation, tilt, shading, and irradiance → estimated PV capacity, annual energy, and CO₂ offset — then aggregate to neighbourhood choropleths with an equity lens.

Built entirely from public data and APIs, modelled in open-source Python, and delivered as an interactive web map. The Esri stack is one supported deployment target, not a dependency — see the appendix.

03

The core design insight: building resolution × city extent

The scale move

A single building is too small to read as "geospatial." The fix is not to abandon building-level detail — it is to keep building-level resolution while operating at city-wide extent, then aggregate up.

That is precisely what Google Project Sunroof and NREL/NLR rooftop-potential studies do: score tens of thousands of individual roofs across a metro, then roll up to neighbourhood / census-tract choropleths for planning and equity analysis. You get both the per-roof craft and the real urban-scale geospatial engineering — large data volumes, tiling, spatial joins, aggregation, spatial statistics.

05

Two modelling modes behind one interface

The geometry front-end is swappable; the rest of the pipeline (radiation → yield → aggregation) is identical downstream. This is what makes the same codebase serve a data-rich US city and a sparse-data Indian one — and it is a direct descendant of the prototype.

High-resolution path — LiDAR per-roof V1 default

Clip the LiDAR point cloud per footprint, fit individual roof planes (RANSAC baseline or the ML route in §9), and resolve genuine per-roof slope, aspect, and obstructions. This is the Washington-DC v1 default and the source of the project's resolution story.

Block-model path — LOD-1 extruded footprints Heritage + fallback

Extrude footprints to a single height per building (the LOD-1 model my SIH prototype ran on). No roof-plane detail, but it still supports full inter-building shading and a flat-roof / fixed-tilt yield estimate. This mode is a first-class citizen, not an afterthought: it is both the honest fallback wherever LiDAR is unavailable (§13, India) and the direct continuation of the prototype's approach — proof the architecture degrades gracefully instead of breaking.

Design payoff

Framing LOD-1 as a mode rather than a limitation turns the prototype's constraint into an engineering feature: one provider-abstracted pipeline, two resolution regimes, an explicit and documented accuracy trade-off between them.

07

Technical pipeline

  1. Footprints — pull building polygons for the study area (Microsoft Buildings or OSM).
  2. DSM from LiDAR — PDAL pipeline: readers.ept → ground filter (filters.smrf) → writers.gdal output_type:max for the surface (buildings + canopy); min gives bare-earth DTM. Keep the DSM — every building at its real height — this is what makes inter-building shading possible (§7).
  3. Solar radiation on the surface — compute per-cell insolation with shadowing. Three real options, no free lunch: (a) GRASS r.sun (open-source, scriptable from Python; horizon/shadowing via r.horizon) — recommended for the raster surface; (b) ArcGIS Raster Solar Radiation (the "Esri way", Appendix); (c) pvlib for point-based plane-of-array irradiance. There is no pure-Python raster solar model — plan on GRASS for the surface pass and pvlib for the final per-plane estimate. Decide this up front.
  4. Roof-plane / orientation / tilt extraction — clip points per footprint, then either RANSAC plane-fitting (classical baseline, same approach as Project Sunroof) or the ML route (§9). In block-model mode (§5) this step is skipped in favour of the extruded height.
  5. Filter usable roof area — adopt a documented baseline rather than inventing thresholds: drop slope > 45°, radiation < 800 kWh/m²/yr, and north-facing surfaces (N. hemisphere); aggregate per building via zonal statistics. Because the radiation raster already carries shading, a heavily shaded roof falls below the threshold automatically.
  6. PV capacity & annual energy — feed usable area + tilt + azimuth and the shading-adjusted insolation from step 3 into PVWatts v8 → annual kWh, capacity factor; derive CO₂ offset from a grid-emissions factor. See §7 for why the shading value must pass through here, not raw location irradiance.
  7. Aggregate — spatial-join per-building results into census tracts → choropleths of potential capacity, generation, and (with demographic joins) an equity view.
08

Inter-building & mutual shading — a first-class effect

In dense areas, shadows cast by neighbouring buildings are often the single largest correction to raw roof potential — bigger than tilt or orientation. My SIH prototype already modelled this (shadows cast across the Delhi block); this project preserves that rigour at city scale, but only because of one deliberate design choice worth stating explicitly.

The mechanism

We model on a DSM — the full elevation surface, every building at its real height plus trees and terrain — not on isolated per-roof geometry. When the raster solar-radiation model runs over that DSM it performs horizon / shadow-casting per cell: for each point on a roof it tests whether a neighbour (or the roof's own parapet/dormer) blocks the sun along its path. Integrated across the day and year, this captures the time-varying reality — the tall building to the south shades your roof at 9 am in December but not at noon in June.

So height differences and adjacency are accounted for — via the DSM feeding steps 3→6. But this only works if the following are handled; each is a real trap:

  1. Use the DSM, never the DTM, for the radiation pass. The bare-earth DTM has no buildings, so it zeroes out all inter-building shading.
  2. Buffer the study area. A building on the AOI's south edge is shaded by a taller one just outside it. Pad the DSM by a margin sized to the tallest plausible shadow-caster, or edge buildings read as over-optimistic.
  3. Set an adequate shadow search distance. Both r.sun / r.horizon and the ArcGIS tools cap how far they look for obstructions; downtown, a far tall tower still matters, so default radii can under-capture.
  4. Don't let PVWatts silently undo it. PVWatts assumes an unshaded horizon. Feed it the shading-adjusted per-roof insolation (or convert to a shading-loss factor) — not the raw NSRDB location value, which discards the shading you just computed.
  5. Trees and seasonality are only partial. A single leaf-on LiDAR/NAIP capture models summer tree shadows but not deciduous leaf-off in winter — a known, documentable bias.
  6. Resolution sets the ceiling. At ~1 m LiDAR DSM you resolve building edges and genuine inter-building shadows. A coarse (e.g. 30 m) DEM cannot — one building shading its neighbour is sub-pixel. This directly bounds the block-model / India path (§5, §13).

Interview value: "here's how I handle mutual shading in dense blocks, and here's the subtle trap where PVWatts quietly discards it" is exactly the second-order reasoning that separates a modeller from a tutorial-follower.

09

Where the ML actually lives

Two legitimate options — do the first for depth, or start with the second and add the first as a stretch:

  • Deep-learning roof-plane & obstruction segmentation. Train / fine-tune on RoofN3D (TU Berlin, free LiDAR roof-plane dataset); reference modern architectures (RoofSeg, SPPSFormer for point-cloud roof-plane instance segmentation; U-Net / Mask R-CNN for imagery). Segmenting obstructions (chimneys, vents, HVAC) that eat usable area is where accuracy — and an architectural eye — pays off.
  • Learned suitability / ranking model. Gradient boosting or a small NN over engineered features (slope, aspect, irradiance, usable area, self-shading), trained against PVWatts- or Sunroof-style labels. Simpler to ship, still a defensible ML story, and pairs naturally with SHAP-style feature attribution for interpretability.
10

Vertical-facade BIPV Stretch goal

The SIH prototype scored solar potential on vertical facades, not just rooftops — the "BI" in BIPV. Rooftop-only would silently drop that ambition, so it stays on the map as an explicit, clearly-scoped stretch rather than a v1 promise.

The honest engineering note is what makes it interesting: LiDAR under-samples vertical surfaces (it is a broadly top-down capture), so the high-resolution path is actually the weaker route for facades. The block-model path (§5) is the more tractable one — extruded LOD-1 walls give clean vertical surfaces, and the same DSM radiation pass already computes irradiance on them. This is a neat inversion: the prototype-heritage mode is the natural home for the prototype's most distinctive feature. Scope: facades as a Phase-2+ add-on on the block-model path, with per-facade irradiance from the raster surface and a documented accuracy caveat versus rooftops.

Phased roadmap

Phase 0 · Scoping & de-risking — a few evenings

Pick the study area (recommended: Washington, DC — dense open LiDAR, and a well-documented reference workflow exists for a DC neighbourhood to benchmark against). Confirm 3DEP coverage/vintage, get the NLR API key, stand up the repo and a data-access smoke test per source. Decide GRASS-vs-ArcGIS for the radiation step now.

Phase 1 · MVP — one neighbourhood, end-to-end · 2–3 weekends

One DC neighbourhood. Footprints + DSM + radiation (with shading) + RANSAC roof planes + PVWatts → a per-building suitability score and a clean static map. Explicit goal: reproduce and sanity-check against a published reference. Ship it even if rough — it proves the whole spine works.

Phase 2 · Full — city-scale + ML + deployed app · multi-week

Scale to the full District: tiling/batching for the whole point cloud, the ML segmentation model (RoofN3D-trained) for planes + obstructions, census-tract aggregation, an equity overlay, and the deployed web app. This is the portfolio centrepiece. Facade BIPV (§10) enters here as a stretch.

Phase 3 · India — re-version via modular adapters · stretch

Swap data adapters behind the common interface for an Indian city (§13). Deliberately showcases modular, provider-abstracted design — and the block-model path (§5) that traces straight back to the SIH prototype.

The honest boundary — say it out loud

Footprints, irradiance, and aggregation are true adapter swaps. But DSM and imagery are limited by resolution, not just source — so the India version drops to the block-model path (§5): LOD-1 extruded footprints with a flat-roof / fixed-tilt heuristic, exactly the prototype's original approach. Inter-building shading (§7) is the sharpest casualty: at 30 m a building shading its neighbour is sub-pixel, so mutual shading drops to a coarse sky-view-factor or horizon estimate — state the accuracy hit plainly. Naming this trade-off is the senior move; hiding it is the junior one.

14

Honest risks & caveats

  1. DSM isn't off-the-shelf for all of the US — deriving it from raw 3DEP point clouds is real engineering (compute + volume). Pick a city with confirmed dense, recent LiDAR; lean on Planetary Computer's pre-derived DSM where it exists.
  2. No pure-Python raster solar model — you take a GRASS (install/scripting overhead) or ArcGIS (licensing) dependency for the surface pass. Decide early.
  3. Shading is only as good as the DSM + settings — DTM-instead-of-DSM, an unbuffered AOI, a too-small shadow radius, or passing raw irradiance to PVWatts each silently discards inter-building shading (§7). Easiest mistakes to make, hardest to notice.
  4. Roof-plane extraction is the weakest link — its errors propagate straight into capacity/kWh/CO₂. No large public ground-truth for arbitrary US cities (RoofN3D is NYC-specific), so validating a new city is nontrivial. Report uncertainty; don't over-claim precision.
  5. Cloud/free-tier limits are real — hosted-layer publishing is often credit- or quota-metered; batch publishes and confirm caps before committing a platform as the final target.
  6. India ≠ "just swap the adapter" — the geometry/imagery/shading stages need a functional downgrade to the block-model path, not a repoint. Scope it as such.

Appendix A

The Esri deployment track

The pipeline above is vendor-neutral. Esri is a first-class supported deployment target and, for a GeoAI role, the most direct signal of fit — collected here so it stays decoupled from the core.

  • Publish results: turn the GeoDataFrame into a Spatially Enabled DataFrame and push a hosted feature layer — sdf.spatial.to_featurelayer(title=..., gis=gis) (supports overwrite=True for refreshes).
  • Interactive deliverable: ArcGIS Online Web Map → wrap in an Instant App (no-code) or Experience Builder (low-code).
  • "Esri way" modelling: do the radiation with Raster Solar Radiation (upward hemispherical viewshed per cell, so surrounding buildings occlude the sky — note the legacy Area Solar Radiation tool is being superseded) and the segmentation with arcgis.learn (15+ built-in DL models on PyTorch/fastai) inside ArcGIS Pro — end-to-end ecosystem fluency.
  • Benchmark: Esri's "Estimate solar power potential" tutorial (Glover Park, DC) — DSM + footprints → Raster Solar Radiation → slope/aspect filter → zonal stats — is the Phase-1 template and reference; plus Esri's public "Rooftop Solar Panel Suitability" Experience Builder app as a deliverable model.
  • Free-tier caution: ArcGIS Online Public/Personal accounts are credit-metered per republish (2 GB storage). A city-scale layer fits, but repeated re-publishing burns credits — batch publishes; check developers.arcgis.com/faq before committing.

Esri-application-specific positioning (the interview narrative and the tailored resume copy) lives in the separate Esri project spec, not here — this document is the vendor-neutral engineering plan for the general portfolio and repo.
